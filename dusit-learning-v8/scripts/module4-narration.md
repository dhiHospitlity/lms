## Slide 2 (s-goldrule). The one rule above every lever

### Reading text

> Before we look at a single lever, there is one rule that sits above all of them, and it is written into every one of the OTA agreements in exactly the same words.
>
> The Dusit Gold member rate must always be the most attractive offer a guest can find.
>
> Every promotional tool you can activate lives below it. Boosters, ads, value-add, member deals, point of sale, packages.
>
> And this is the line you never cross. After all the public and member discounts are combined, no OTA offer may undercut that member rate, for any stay period.
>
> That is why stacking is dangerous. Each discount on its own might be fine, but combined, they can quietly push the OTA price below your own best member rate. Now you are competing against yourself and breaking parity at the same time.
>
> So keep the crown in your head. Every lever you pull has to leave it there. If a promotion would undercut it, that promotion is a no.

## Slide 4 (s-green). The no-price-impact levers

### Reading text

> Start with the three no-price-impact levers, the green group.
>
> First, the ranking booster. You pay the OTA, usually by performance, to rank higher. The pro is you only pay when it works. The risk is that some boosters quietly behave like a discount, so read what you are signing up for.
>
> Second, paid advertising. You buy a top slot outright. The pro is the best ranking with no impact on your ADR, because it is advertising, not a discount. The con is it needs a budget, and there is no guarantee of bookings.
>
> Third, value added. Instead of cutting the rate, you add a perk. A room upgrade, a hotel credit, early check-in, free breakfast. The big pro is it maintains parity, because the price does not move. The con is that not every channel accepts it.
>
> And remember the guardrail. The value you give an OTA guest must not beat what a Dusit Gold member gets. A perk can undercut your own programme just as easily as a price can.

## Slide 5 (s-red). The price-impact levers

### Reading text

> Now the three price-impact levers, the red group. These move the price, so handle them with care.
>
> First, member exclusive. A deal shown only to the OTA's logged-in members, so it does not change your public price. That is the pro. The con is that it competes directly with your own Dusit Gold programme, and can shift guests from your channel to theirs.
>
> Second, point of sale. A discount aimed at one market only, say China or the Middle East. The pro is it protects your public rate while targeting demand where you need it. The con is disparity risk, because every OTA manages point of sale differently, and a deal can leak to guests it was never meant for.
>
> Third, package promo. A lower rate hidden inside a bundle. Flight plus hotel, or a B2B rate plan. The pros are that the low rate is fenced, and cancellations are low. The cons are that it can leak to B2B and metasearch, and it is high cost.
>
> Bundle and fence it before it goes on sale, never after.

## Slide 11 (s-pos-pkg). POS and package

>

>
> The strength of packages is that the low rate is fenced and cancellations are low. The danger is leakage. A packaged rate can escape to B2B and metasearch, where it undercuts everything.

>

>
> On Agoda, the Opaque discount cannot be combined with Agoda Private Sales.
>
> On Booking, you take Genius Level One and stop. Never Level Two or Three.
>
> On Trip.com, you clear endorsed promotions through the market manager first.
>
> The safe default is simple. When in doubt, do not stack. One lever, chosen well, almost always beats three piled on top of each other, because three stacked discounts is exactly how you undercut Dusit Gold without meaning to.

> So how do you choose? Work down a simple ladder, in order, every time.
>
> One. Is the foundation right? The fourteen rate plans set up correctly. If not, fix that first, because no promotion saves a broken base.
>
> Two. Can a green lever solve it? A ranking booster or a paid ad to get seen, or a value-added perk to compete without discounting. Reach here first, because these cannot break parity.
>
> Three. Only if you genuinely need to move price, choose a fenced price-impact lever. A targeted point-of-sale deal or a bundled package. And check that specific OTA's stacking rule before you combine anything.
>
> Four. The final check, always. After everything is applied, does the offer still leave the Dusit Gold member rate as the most attractive? If yes, go. If no, stop.
>
> That ladder turns a pile of tempting levers into one clear decision. Foundation, then green, then fenced price, then the Dusit Gold check. In that order.
