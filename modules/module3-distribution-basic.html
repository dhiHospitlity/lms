B2C and B2B — who is the customer?

B2C · business to consumer
The customer is the guest.
Definition
They see the rate. They book it. They pay it. They turn up.
Examples
Your website, the front desk, and also OTAs — the guest still chooses and pays; the OTA sits in the middle.
B2B · business to business
The customer is a business, not the guest.
Definition
Wholesaler, bedbank, tour operator, DMC.
How it flows
Buys at a net rate, resells to travel agents, and the agent sells to the guest at whatever price the partner chooses.
B2C: you control the guest price. B2B: you control the partner price. After that, it's out of your hands, which is where the problems start.

The line blurred

01
Past
Wholesalers sold room-only rates to trade partners. Guests booked direct. B2B and B2C ran on separate rails.
02
Today
Wholesalers reach guests through connected agents and white-label sites. The lanes cross.
03
Impact
A B2B net rate shows up on a public site below your own price. Your parity is gone.

GDS and metasearch

Two more players in the connected map — both look similar, they do very different jobs.
Sells the room
GDS
Global Distribution System
The network corporate travel agents book on — flights, hotels and cars in one place. Business travel runs on it.
Amadeus Galileo Sabre Travelport
Compares · doesn't sell
Metasearch
Price-comparison layer
Compares prices across channels then sends the guest onward — usually to dusit.com or to an OTA. The transaction happens elsewhere.
Google Hotel Ads Trivago TripAdvisor Kayak
GDS sells the room. Metasearch decides where the guest goes to buy it.
