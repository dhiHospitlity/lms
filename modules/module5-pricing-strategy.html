Section 1 · The cost of discounting

The maths of discounting

Every discount has a break-even point: the extra room nights you must sell just to stand still.

THE FORMULA
Break-even uplift
= Discount % ÷ (1 − Discount %)
Variable cost bites too. At $200 with a $40 room cost, contribution is $160. At $180 it is $140. You need more rooms again to recover the same contribution.

Section 3

Five ways to price a room

01Cost-based
02Demand-based
03Value-based
04Competition-based
05Reference-based

Dusit uses elements of all five, but not equally. Knowing which one you are applying is what separates reactive pricing from rational pricing.

Methods 01 and 02

The floor, and the signal

01 · Cost-based
Room cost → Add mark-up → Minimum rate

Housekeeping, staffing, and a fair allocation of fixed overhead, plus a mark-up. This is your floor. You never price below it, whatever demand does.

Risk: cost plus mark-up with no eye on demand leaves money on the table when the market is strong.
02 · Demand-based
Low demand night$140
High demand night$280

Rate follows the demand signal. Booking pace, competitor occupancy, events in the city. This is the primary method Dusit uses for BAR decisions.

Risk: demand-based pricing with no cost floor slips below break-even in a desperate week.
Methods 03, 04 and 05

Three methods about perception

Not cost. Not demand. What the guest believes.

03 · Value-based

The rate reflects what the guest believes the experience is worth.

A sea-view room beats the same room facing the car park. Not because it costs more to clean.
Risk: overestimate perceived value and the rooms sit empty.
04 · Competition-based

The rate is calibrated against what comparable hotels charge.

Comp set at $200 and you are at $140? You may simply be underpricing your product.
Risk: following competitors into a race to the bottom.
05 · Reference-based

What the guest expects it to cost, based on what they paid before.

They paid $150 last year. They anchor against that when they see $200 today.
Risk: raise without communicating added value and it reads as unfair.
Dusit's blend: demand-based for daily BAR movement, value-based for premium positioning, competition-based for market calibration, cost-based for floor protection, reference-based for how the offer is communicated.

Section 4 · The ascending strategy

Prices go up, not down

Set a low entry rate at thirty days out to build base occupancy. Then raise it in steps as arrival approaches and rooms run short.

Dropping price in the last seven days trains your market to wait. That expectation costs far more, over time, than any single night it rescues.
1
Late bookers are less price-sensitive
They need a room. Scarcity justifies the premium.
2
It prevents cancel-and-rebook
Guests who see prices rising will not gamble on a lower rate later.
3
It builds price confidence
Steady upward movement signals that Dusit rooms hold their value.
4
It protects brand equity
Last-minute discounts teach guests to wait for the deal.
What not to do: drop price in the last seven days to fill rooms. It rarely generates the volume needed to cover the rate cut.
The one exception

Repositioning is not panic pricing

When something removes the demand basis for a period, a cancelled conference, a group pulling out, it is valid to reset. The difference is intention and process.

✓ REPOSITIONING
1Diagnose the real cause of the demand gap.
2Set a new, lower BAR deliberately, with Revenue Manager approval.
3Resume the ascending strategy from the new base.
Deliberate. Approved. Monitored.
✗ PANIC PRICING

A reflexive rate drop because the hotel feels slow. No diagnosis, no approval, no plan for what happens after.

It almost always costs more than it recovers, and it leaves the rate architecture worse than it found it.

Emotional. Unapproved. Unmeasured.
One is strategy. The other is guessing. The rate on the screen can look identical either way, which is exactly why the process matters.

Customer-centric in practice
Pricing is not emotional. It runs on data and proven method, never gut feel.
Perceived value must justify the price the guest sees.
Offers stay simple and clear. Confusion reduces conversion.
Optimisation-focused in practice
Rates must stay yieldable. The Revenue Manager has to be able to move them.
Win-win: a rate that feels fair to the guest and delivers profit to the hotel.
Balance simplicity for the guest, flexibility for the RM, consistency across channels.
Dusit's public rate structure has four rates. Flexible, Advance Saver, Early Bird, Stay Longer. Enough choice for every kind of commitment, without overwhelming the guest into booking somewhere simpler.

Definition

What a package actually is

Accommodation sold in conjunction with other hotel services, as part of a single transaction with the guest.

Room + breakfast

A standard permanent offer, not a promotional package.

Room-only and room-with-breakfast must both be available on all channels at all times. Packages are built on top of this.
Room + add-ons

The true hotel package. Spa, dining, transfers, activities.

This is where property teams have the most influence, and what the rest of this module is about.
Room + transport

OTA-led packages. Expedia and Trip.com bundle your room with flights.

Managed by Corporate Office. The hotel supplies the accommodation component; commercial terms are set centrally.
The framework

Leaders, Fillers, and Killers

LEADER
What the guest is willing to pay a premium for. Without it, the package does not convert.
Signature restaurant dinner · Spa treatment · Airport transfer
FILLER
Nice to have. Adds perceived value at low marginal cost, but does not drive the booking on its own.
Breakfast · Late checkout · Welcome amenity · Bottle of champagne
KILLER
Something the guest does not want, or that reads as a hidden charge. One killer can override every leader and filler.
Pet supplement · Early check-in fee · Parking surcharge · Mandatory gala dinner
Good package = leaders + fillers. Never killers.
Maximum three add-ons. More than that and the offer stops being easy to sell.
The framework that keeps it coherent

Package rules and golden rules

Package pricing rules
1Minimum +10% premium over the equivalent room-only or room-with-breakfast rate.
2Discount the add-ons, never the room rate.
3Use dynamic BAR-linked pricing. Never a static package rate.
4Match the booking lead time to the package conditions.
5No lower rate than dusit.com on any online third-party channel, ever.
